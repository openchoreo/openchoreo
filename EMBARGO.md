# OpenChoreo Security Embargo Policy

## 1. Application of this Policy

This Policy applies to the maintainers, the Security Team and the pre-notification list members who are using this project.

## 2. Confidentiality

Anyone holding embargoed information (which is any privileged security information) such as maintainers, the Security Team, and pre-notification list members; must not make it public, share it, or hint at it, except with the explicit approval of the Security Team. This obligation continues until the agreed public disclosure date, and covers the vulnerability, the fix, and anything the vulnerability could be derived from.

Before sharing embargoed information with colleagues who need it to prepare a fix, make sure they agree to these same terms. Keep that group as small as the work allows.

## 3. Leaks

If embargoed information is shared with anyone who should not have it by accident or otherwise, contact security@openchoreo.dev immediately upon becoming aware of the leak, by replying to the original disclosure thread. Say what was disclosed, to whom, and when. Do this before trying to contain it yourself; we need to know in order to decide whether the public disclosure date must move.

## 4. Breach

Reporting a leak promptly is not itself a breach and it keeps your membership in good standing.

The following are breaches:

- Failing to report a leak as soon as you become aware of it
- Repeated leaks, including leaks through parties you shared with
- Deliberate disclosure before the agreed date
- Distributing a fix outside the terms of (6)

A first inadvertent leak, promptly reported, normally results in a retrospective and tightened handling. Repeated or deliberate breaches result in removal from the pre-notification list or the Security Team, and replacement. Removal of the membership is noted publicly, with the reason. The organisation who is the member will be responsible for all actions of individuals acting on behalf of the organisation.

Where a leak is material, we may shorten the embargo or disclose the vulnerability to the public before the 30 day default disclosure period, whether or not a member is removed.

## 5. Disclosure timeline

Report privately through GitHub private vulnerability reporting or security@openchoreo.dev. We acknowledge within 3 business days.

Default public disclosure is 30 days from validation by the Security Team. The default 30 day period may be extended, up to a maximum of 90 days, where the fix is complex or the expertise needed is unavailable. In the event the vulnerability has been identified by a reporter we will inform the reporter of the public disclosure date and the reasons for any extended disclosure dates. Actively exploited issues are disclosed to the public as soon as a fix or mitigation exists without adhering to the 30–90 day disclosure periods.

## 6. Embargo list

OpenChoreo maintains a private pre-notification list so that downstream distributors can prepare fixes, and their users are protected when the vulnerability is disclosed rather than weeks later.

Organisations that distribute OpenChoreo, or a product derived from it, to third parties and wish to become members may apply to security@openchoreo.dev with a monitored role address and written acceptance of this policy. Membership is published.

Members are notified once the fix is available, and receive the candidate patch and the planned disclosure date.

Patches are distributed to the members. Membership grants no access to OpenChoreo repositories or draft advisories.

Everything a member receives is embargoed information under (2) until the disclosure date.

## 7. Legal obligations

Complying with a legal duty, such as reporting to a regulator under the EU Cyber Resilience Act is not a breach. Tell us you are filing, and disclose only what is required.
